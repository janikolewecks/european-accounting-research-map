Where the data come from

The corpus is the papers accepted for presentation at the Annual Congress of the European Accounting Association in nine congress years, 2015 to 2018 and 2022 to 2026. Comparable records are not available for 2019 to 2021, so those years are absent rather than empty. The unit is one accepted paper with a usable abstract; papers that were submitted but not accepted are not part of it. Eighty-eight papers appear at two congresses under the same title and primary author and are counted once, at their first appearance. A paper’s country is the country of its primary author’s affiliation.

How the labels are made

Large language models read each abstract and assign labels from a fixed vocabulary of 25 topics, 14 method families and 8 data sources, with the same prompts and settings in every congress year, so movement in a series reflects the research rather than a change of coding. Topics and methods are multi-label and their shares do not sum to 100; the data source is single-label. The method labels were checked against the codes authors chose for themselves on the EAA submission form: across the 6,013 papers carrying both, the two agree in 87.2 percent of cases, with a Cohen’s kappa of 0.739. The labels are ours, not the authors’ own.

What the accounting families are

Countries are grouped in the Nobes tradition (Nobes 1983, 1998, 2006), which sorts national accounting environments by historically persistent institutional differences rather than by geography. A family aggregates every country assigned to it, including those too small to appear on their own in the country filter.     What this page publishes, and how to reuse it

    The file behind this page holds six numbers for each paper: congress year, author country, topic and method labels, data source and evidence country. No names, no titles, no abstracts, no affiliations and nothing at the level of an institution. The build refuses to publish if any of those ever reach a file a visitor receives. The congress programmes are public, so a rare combination of labels could in principle be traced back to a paper; the tables print the base of every row so that small cells are not over-read. Figures can differ from the published paper where it applies further sample restrictions.
